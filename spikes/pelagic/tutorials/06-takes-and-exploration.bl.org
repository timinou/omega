#+title: Capture, fork, and explore a performance
#+status: BL model verified; browser workflow acceptance in progress

* Make one change safely
Start Pelagic with =bl pelagic.dev= from the project root. Choose PERFORM, edit
the source below, then AUDITION CODE. The quiet monitor previews the proposed
state while the active performance keeps running. COMMIT AT BAR accepts it;
DISCARD PREVIEW leaves the active revision alone.
#+begin_src beam-lisp
(av/variation {:seed 37 :density 0.7 :transpose -7})
#+end_src

* Capture a reproducible state timeline
CAPTURE TAKE records the starting document, session, transport, asset references,
and subsequent state/controller checkpoints. FINISH TAKE closes capture.
SAVE TAKE exports the manifest. REPLAY restores the starting session and queues
its checkpoints by their captured musical beat. Imported sample assets remain
content-addressed references; another machine must relink those assets.
The manifest is bounded to 512 events and 2 MiB. An overflow is explicitly
incomplete and cannot be presented as a reproducible take.

FORK SOURCE turns the final captured state into an editable =av/commit= block
in the real editor. This is one undoable edit. Audition the fork before committing.
A take manifest is distinct from the audiovisual WebM recording; both can be
kept together, but a recording is not an editable performance account.

* Explore from a fixed base
Enter a seed and choose EXPLORE. Four alternatives derive deterministically
from that seed and the current revision. Choose one to audition it. Generate
again after changing the active state: a stale exploration cannot commit over
an intervening edit. Save a snapshot before a longer exploration if you want a
named return point.

The core of exploration is ordinary pure BL, not another browser randomizer:
#+begin_src beam-lisp
(require '[pelagic.explore :as explore])
(explore/candidates {:variation {:seed 37}} 37 4)
#+end_src
Metadata generation is bounded to eight small alternatives; expensive score
preparation remains owned work in the shared actor worker. Actor turns and
playback reserve admission protect audio from preparation bursts.
