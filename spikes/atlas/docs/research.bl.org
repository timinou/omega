* The revised question
ArtCraft is a demonstrator of a possible market movement, not the company
whose commercial success has to displace Adobe. The hypothesis is that
technically informed people can see the reproduction of complicated software
and familiar workflows becoming dramatically easier while investors still
value some application subscriptions as durable, difficult-to-contest rents.
Open implementations, competing forks, custom software and future entrants
could transmit that change even if ArtCraft itself disappears.

The earlier analysis imposed too much of a company-displacement sequence:
attention, parity, paid migration, financial damage, then a trade. That is
incomplete for an information event. Investors can immediately change their
beliefs about future pricing power, terminal growth or the duration of cash
flows. A multiple can compress while this quarter's subscriptions still grow.
Reported churn is a later fundamental test, not a prerequisite for repricing.

The corrected question is: what has technical evidence newly revealed about
contestable software rents; who understands the implication; what does the
market already discount; and what event could close the remaining belief gap?

* Two clocks, two theses
** Fast clock / information and valuation
A demonstration makes a previously abstract possibility tangible. Independent
reproduction makes it credible. Creators and operators translate it into
workflow implications. Financial interpreters translate those implications
into estimates of pricing power and business duration. Investors can respond
to that interpretation before anyone has cancelled a subscription.

An information-asymmetry trade needs a difference between the analyst's
understanding and market expectations, plus some route to convergence. It
cannot be inferred from excitement, a missing headline in one search, or a
flat stock chart. Investors may already know the argument, assign it a low
probability, expect incumbent adaptation, or price a substantial risk premium.
Those alternatives require investigation rather than an assumption of ignorance.

** Slow clock / economic realization
Over time, repeated use, renewals, negotiated prices, conversion rates, gross
margins and product investment test whether the expected rent erosion occurs.
These observations matter for fundamental conviction and eventual valuation.
They are not the only signals that can move a stock first.

The two clocks can disagree. A sharp selloff can overestimate substitution.
Strong current earnings can coexist with a rational fall in terminal value.
An excellent technology forecast can produce a poor short when the stock has
already discounted it or convergence takes longer than the financing horizon.

* What ArtCraft would newly demonstrate
The central mechanism has two parts. First, AI assistance may lower the cost
and time of implementing a substantial application surface. Second, reproducing
familiar interactions may reduce the learned-behavior component of switching.
The latter is more specific than the existence of free alternatives. A user
can face no license fee and still face substantial retraining costs.

These are hypotheses with distinct tests. Visible breadth across applications
is not independently measured implementation cost. Interface familiarity is
not file fidelity, plug-in compatibility, color accuracy, team coordination,
cloud identity, procurement or support. The maintainer's alpha status remains
important evidence about depth, without making commercial alpha status a veto
on the broader information thesis.

Clean-room is an attributed development claim, not an independently established
methodology or legal judgment. The replicability argument should survive an
honest accounting of engineering work, maintenance, licensing, security and
interoperability. One repository is not a statistically representative sample
of how cheaply all software can now be built.

* DeepSeek / same epistemic structure, different exposures
The January 20, 2025 R1 release directly contested beliefs about proprietary
frontier reasoning capability, including the competitive positions of OpenAI
and Anthropic. Open weights, API access and inspectable claims allowed people
to try and discuss an alternative rather than merely imagine one.

The January 27 financial shock was an additional event. Nvidia's observed
share-price loss is a listed-market transmission into expectations for AI
compute and capital expenditure. It is not a measurement of OpenAI's or
Anthropic's equity loss, nor proof that an AI-model entrant took Nvidia users.
A single shock can have different implications for model margins, suppliers,
cloud distributors and application consumers of cheaper intelligence.

For software, the analogous scarce asset is implementation effort and durable
user dependence on established applications. Replication evidence could change
expected rents without a single entrant winning the market. The analogy is a
sequence of belief changes, not an instruction to copy Nvidia's percentage loss
onto Adobe. Different priors, exposures, timing and complementary assets matter.

The release date was a US market holiday. The daily-tape release anchor therefore
starts at January 21, the first captured trading session on or after January 20.
A six-session release window overlaps the January 27 shock; these are alternative
anchors for one episode, not independent observations that can be pooled.

* The essential prior-belief control
The broad AI application-layer threat was financial-market news months before
ArtCraft. Reuters's February 4 account describes investors reconsidering software
businesses following Anthropic's new tool. This is evidence against a thesis
that the entire software-replacement idea remains hidden from financial markets.
It also supplies a closer listed-software repricing comparison than Nvidia alone.

A later financial-sector commentary reports substantial prior compression in
creative-software valuation medians. The desk displays that publisher's bucket
table with its date, composition limitations and a small table/prose discrepancy.
Those bucket medians are not Adobe's multiple or a direct measure of Adobe's
implied disruption probability.

The remaining possible edge is narrower and more interesting: perhaps the
market understands agent automation but underestimates broad replication of
familiar standalone applications; perhaps it prices seat compression but misses
falling switching costs; perhaps repeated independent implementations change
beliefs about the speed and generality of the process. None is established by
assertion. Each needs comparison with actual public investor expectations.

These are selected historical comparisons, not causal estimates. The first

The second is evidence that listed software had already experienced a repricing

The third shows an Adobe decline on the declared date; it does not attribute
that decline to ArtCraft or establish a persistent correction.

The current episode has only two captured sessions from October 7 through
October 8. Adobe's modeled cumulative QQQ residual over those sessions is about
+0.21%; the price rebound prevents a simple persistent-selloff reading. Current
volume is also much less extreme than Nvidia's January 27 volume spike. This
is contextual evidence about the tape, not proof that investors ignored a
technology signal. The earlier October 5–8 window remains available as a
window-sensitivity comparison; it cannot be silently substituted for the new
anchor to manufacture a preferred conclusion.

The desk shows both raw QQQ/IGV day differentials and an OLS model fitted on
80 matched pre-event sessions. CAR is the arithmetic sum of daily residuals
from session zero. The pre-event model contains no event or post-event returns.
Volume is divided by the previous 20-session average. These diagnostics make
assumptions visible; they do not identify a treatment effect.

The current Adobe/QQQ fitted beta is negative (about -0.46), illustrating why
one recent beta estimate must not become false precision. Report raw and sector
comparisons beside residuals. No standard errors, significance, intraday timing,
placebo analysis or co-occurring earnings/rates controls are claimed. Some press
arrived after US trading hours; the date anchor describes a press cluster, not a
uniform intraday information arrival. Later events can contaminate longer paths.

Two public-source annotations further constrain attribution. October 7 AP
reporting describes broad equity and Treasury-yield pressure (E18), so a common
discount-rate shock is a competing explanation. Adobe's September 3 succession
announcement (E19) is background within the beta-fit period, not an October 7
catalyst. Neither annotation is part of the eight-document Jev corpus.

* News sentiment / typed interpretation, not a market oracle
Eight documents were selected deliberately across technical, creative and
financial channels. This is a small panel to inspect competing framings, not a
news census or a statistical estimate of market sentiment. HN publication cohorts
remain visible separately; historical point totals are not historical daily growth.

Jev answered three closed questions for each public input: framing toward
incumbent rents, dominant economic mechanism, and whether the text reports
realized repricing, prospective risk or neither. This separates positive
enthusiasm about an open tool from negative implications for incumbent economics.
The exact questions, requested/returned model versions, probabilities and input
hashes are retained. The model does not validate a clean-room claim, infer a
market consensus, or estimate an Adobe-return probability.

Five inputs are extracted article text. Direct HTTP requests to AP, Phoronix
and PetaPixel returned 403. Those failures are retained; their classifications
use separately identified analyst abstracts of web-verified reports. Abstract
framing can introduce analyst bias and must not be pooled invisibly with text.
All three abstract-based tone outputs are below the declared 0.60 review threshold.
They abstain from aggregate counts. The AP abstract even receives a low-confidence
supportive label despite describing a selloff: a concrete example of why the
review queue matters. Do not repair this by silently changing the model output.

In the five article-text inputs, R1's announcement and creator-sector coverage
are classified as threatening to incumbent rents; the financial commentaries
are mixed. That describes this panel's framing, not the proportion of investors
who are bearish. Financial reporting can acknowledge severe disruption while
also discussing complements, adaptation or excessive pessimism. Such mixed
framing is analytically useful and should not be forced into a single fear score.

* Implications for Adobe / revise the decision, not just the rhetoric
The company-watch framing is superseded by an active software-rent repricing
thesis. Independent paid migration is no longer a necessary early timing gate.
The current short decision nevertheless remains unresolved because incremental
surprise, current Adobe-specific valuation, convergence catalyst and executable
borrow economics are not established by these observations.

The desk now separates a multiple-reset identity from the slower ARR bridge.
Holding earnings constant, moving an assumed P/E from 24 to 18 implies a 25%
price decline. These are illustrative assumptions, not measured Adobe multiples
or a price target. Earnings changes interact multiplicatively with the multiple
ratio; adding the two component percentages is generally wrong. The separate
short payoff model still includes assumed financing and upside scenarios.

Highest-value next work:
1. Predeclare a broader technical/creator/financial publisher panel and fixed
   date range; identify first-observed independent evidence rather than repeat
   headlines. Compare with dated investor commentary and earnings-call language.
2. Independently reproduce several applications and measure build time, cost,
   maintenance, file fidelity and same-user task completion. Separate lower
   learning friction from the other components of switching.
3. Reconstruct public Adobe-specific valuation and estimate revisions; compare
   reasonable rent-duration scenarios with what the current price implies.
4. Add matched placebo dates, intraday publication times and competing-event
   annotations to the event study before making any causal claim.
5. If a trade reaches review, obtain actual borrow/recall terms, a loss budget,
   a time horizon and an exit rule. Technology insight and executable P&L are
   different questions.
