#+TITLE: Atlas / research dossier — replication, diffusion and Adobe
#+DATE: 2026-10-09
#+STATUS: Public evidence snapshot; conditional research decision

* Editorial decision
Keep Adobe on a disruption watch. The available evidence does not justify an
Artcraft-only short. This is a conclusion about the supplied evidence and the
thesis, not a personalized allocation recommendation. No trade is connected.

The actionable work is to establish production capability, paid substitution,
materiality and an expectation gap. The dashboard makes these four separate
questions. A negative answer at one link can change the decision even when
public attention keeps growing.

* What is being compared
Artcraft is an umbrella with a generative studio and open-source creative apps.
Do not conflate the paid studio's pricing with the desktop apps' licensing.
The object of this case is the open creative-app suite, especially PhotoCraft.

DeepSeek R1 is the January 20, 2025 reasoning-model release. The January 27
market shock is a separate event. R1 has later updates, and a current repository
star count cannot be used as its January 2025 adoption observation.

* Reporting method and evidence classes
Public observations are collected with bounded unauthenticated HTTP functions.
The notebook records original URLs, retrieval timestamps and SHA-256 hashes.
The acquisition manifest preserves source responses; derived values are
recomputed with deterministic functions. Editorial interpretation is written
separately and a generative adversarial review is retained as model output.

The evidence room distinguishes:
- Primary: issuer disclosures, maintainer statements and public API records.
- Secondary: dated original reporting used to establish narrative diffusion.
- Gap: a required observation not present in this research package.
- Inference: an explicit claim connecting observations to an economic mechanism.
- Assumption: a subjective probability, payoff or implementation input.

A primary source can still be promotional. A repository README is strong
evidence of what maintainers say, weaker evidence of product quality, and no
evidence of customers abandoning another product. A press headline can prove
that a narrative crossed an audience boundary without proving the headline's
strongest economic implication.

* The first story: replication changes the supply side
The economically interesting hypothesis is that AI lowers the cost of
recreating parts of existing software. If that is durable, familiar feature
surfaces become less scarce. Open implementations can be forked and adapted,
which may change the bargaining power of users even before incumbent revenue
falls.

This hypothesis has several distinct mechanisms. Entry-level creators could
avoid buying their first subscription. Existing solo users could downgrade.
Agencies could negotiate discounts. An ecosystem could form around open
formats and automation. Developers could use cheaper creation tools to expand
the market rather than displace existing purchases. These mechanisms have
different lags and different implications for Adobe's cash flows.

The maintainer's own status statement is important counterevidence: PhotoCraft
is described as an early alpha, with acknowledged professional workflow gaps.
This is not a finding from independent testing. It is a reason to prioritize
that testing before translating software availability into a revenue forecast.

* The second story: diffusion changes investor attention
A hidden signal becomes useful when its meaning changes across audiences.
A repository can attract builders. A hacker discussion can explain why it is
surprising. Photography and design publications can carry it to potential
users. Financial discussion can turn it into a stock narrative.

The current HN sample is a keyword sample, not a census. Two queries are
combined and deduplicated by story ID. Story publication dates are known,
but scores are observed only at retrieval. A chart of points grouped by story
publication date is therefore a publication-cohort chart. It is not a chart
of points accumulated each day. One star snapshot cannot establish star
velocity or acceleration. Repeated comparable snapshots are needed.

Specialist reporting on October 7 establishes a useful audience-crossing
marker. It does not establish a cancellation cohort or a professional quality
benchmark. Several outlets repeating a developer's claim remain one underlying
claim. The desk should let an analyst trace this dependence rather than count
each repetition as independent validation.

* DeepSeek: what the analogy contributes
The R1 release challenged beliefs about how expensive valuable reasoning
capability had to be. Runnable weights and an API made experimentation
possible. The narrative had implications for an already prominent AI
infrastructure investment theme. That is why the economic mechanism matters
more than the superficial similarity of two viral open-source announcements.

Contemporaneous AP reporting describes a 16.9% Nvidia decline on January 27,
with Nasdaq down 3.1% and the S&P 500 down 1.5%. Many stocks rose and the Dow
gained, so the shock was not a uniform collapse of all equities. The captured
price series independently supports a roughly 17% NVDA daily decline.

The frequently repeated low training-cost number should not be treated as a
complete audited R1 development cost. Training compute, prior research,
distillation, post-training and inference have different economics. This
notebook does not use a disputed cost headline as an input to a valuation.

The relevant analogy is an expectations shock about scarcity. The invalid
analogy is to apply Nvidia's one-day percentage loss to Adobe. The businesses,
valuation expectations, exposed cash flows, demand elasticities and switching
costs differ. One selected historical episode also supplies no calibrated
base rate for future disruption events.

* Adobe: establish the denominator before applying a haircut
Adobe's September 10 issuer release reports $6.76bn quarterly revenue,
$27.50bn ending ARR and $2.52bn operating cash flow for a quarter ending
August 28. These figures precede the Artcraft episode. They establish scale
and pre-event business performance, not proof about future substitution.

A clone of a creative application does not expose all corporate revenue.
The economic bridge needs a cohort denominator: which product, which paid
user segment, which geography and which renewal horizon? Apply migration
and pricing assumptions only to the exposed share. Then model contribution
margin, response costs, buybacks and valuation separately.

The desk intentionally lacks an invented exposed-revenue percentage. The
absence is a research result. Treating all ARR as Photoshop-like revenue
would create a precise answer to the wrong question.

* Market study and practical limits
The data pipeline captures public adjusted daily closes from Yahoo's chart
endpoint. Its manifest records the full parameterized endpoint and response
hash. NVDA and QQQ are compared over January 27, 2025. ADBE and QQQ are compared
over October 5–8, 2026. The latter is a declared narrative-observation window,
not a uniquely identified release event.

The calculation uses compounded buy-and-hold asset return minus compounded
benchmark return. Beta is fixed at one. A positive difference is not evidence
of positive causal impact; a negative difference is not proof of disruption.
A model estimated over a pre-event sample, sector controls, intraday timing,
placebo windows and explicit confounds would improve attribution.

The captured Adobe window does not show a crash comparable to Nvidia's.
This weakens a claim that such a crash has already occurred. It does not rule
out future substitution or prove that investors have correctly priced it.

* A decision model that reveals its assumptions
The scenario sandbox has three analyst-defined future stock-return states.
Probabilities sum to one and are not empirical frequencies. Short P&L per
notional dollar is negative stock return minus annual borrow times holding
years minus friction. Expected P&L is the probability-weighted outcome.

The worst listed scenario is not the maximum loss. Stock upside is unbounded;
borrow can change or be recalled; forced liquidation makes path matter. The
sandbox omits several implementation costs and labels them. A broker quote
is required before interpreting the result as executable economics.

The Bayesian sensitivity panel is arithmetic over a subjective prior and
combined likelihood ratio. It is useful for exposing how strongly a person
must interpret the evidence to change a belief. It is not a calibrated
forecast. Correlated attention signals must not be multiplied as if they
were independent experiments.

* Highest value next investigations
** 1. Professional workflow validation
Pre-register representative tasks using public project assets. Compare
completion time, fidelity, failures and interoperability. Blind judges to
application when scoring outputs. Include plug-ins, color management and
round-trip files. Report failed tasks, not only attractive screenshots.

** 2. Paying-user displacement
Define a denominator of current paying Adobe users. Track repeated usage and
actual cancellation or downgrade, with consent and aggregate reporting.
Separate first-time hobbyists from paid-seat substitution. Intent to cancel
is weaker evidence than a documented billing change.

** 3. Revenue bridge and expectations
Build ranges for exposed share, migration and realization lag. Test what
change in cash-flow expectations is needed to justify the proposed payoff.
A threatened company can already be cheaply priced; a growing company can
still disappoint high expectations. A short thesis needs the difference.

** 4. Trade implementation and reversal rules
Obtain fresh borrow economics and a dated catalyst. Define the maximum
portfolio loss, monitoring frequency and thesis invalidation observations.
Keep decision records append-only or versioned, so later outcomes do not
rewrite what was believed at entry.

* What would change the current decision
Move toward human trade review if independent production tests succeed,
credible paid migration appears, exposed economics are material relative to
expectations, and a bounded implementation fits a separately defined risk
budget. Abandon this specific catalyst thesis if substitutes fail routine
workflows, adoption remains non-paying curiosity, or incumbent responses
absorb the value while pricing already reflects the threat.

* Final audit / public sample and market context
The retained HN sample contains fourteen candidates: twelve suite-related
posts and two studio posts under the title rule used in collect.mjs. The
suite cohort sums to 416 retrieval-time points. The studio records remain
visible and labeled in the source map but do not enter the suite chart.
This is a small biased search sample, not total audience reach.

The matched twenty-session scan ending October 8 shows that Adobe and
Salesforce underperformed QQQ over a broader interval, while Autodesk
outperformed. This is useful context for alternative explanations: broad
software expectations can differ from the selected four-session Artcraft
window. No causal claim follows from these comparative returns. The saved
raw responses and deterministic metrics permit independent reproduction.

The Gen-backed Omega experiment memo proposes an independent production
trial. Its numeric pass/fail thresholds are model suggestions, not empirical
standards; they should be agreed and pre-registered before testing. The Jev
output classifies evidence readiness. Neither artifact supplies a validated
probability that Adobe's price will fall.

* Competitive baseline / ask what is actually new
Free alternatives were available before this episode. GIMP's official 3.0
release was published on March 16, 2025. Blackmagic's current Resolve page
offers a free edition alongside Studio. Their existence does not establish
professional parity or Adobe seat loss, but it changes the right experiment.
The incremental hypothesis is AI-accelerated replication and reduced migration
friction, not simply the existence of a zero-price creative tool.

Include existing alternatives in the proposed workflow trial. If Artcraft
performs no better at migration, fidelity or task completion, the novelty
story may have more attention than incremental economic content. If it improves
those outcomes substantially, the capability mechanism deserves more weight.
This is a falsifiable comparison; no competitive market-share figure is
assumed here.
