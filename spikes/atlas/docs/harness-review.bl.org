#+TITLE: Atlas memo — highest-value next public observation for Artcraft × Adobe

* Recommendation
Seek one public observation: a neutral, pre-registered, blind production-workflow trial of Artcraft/PhotoCraft against Adobe on original professional files, published with raw results and limitations. This is the notebook’s “highest decision relevance” experiment and directly tests the capability gate that gates every later claim ([[E01]], [[E10]]).

* Why this observation
The current case establishes attention, not substitution. HN snapshots, repository counts and specialist press show diffusion across developer and creative audiences ([[E03]], [[E04]], [[E05]]). They do not show that a paying professional can replace Photoshop/Premiere/Lightroom. The only direct product-status evidence is the maintainers’ own alpha caveat: PhotoCraft is “not yet a Photoshop replacement for daily professional work,” with gaps in generative features, tools, typography, professional workflows and plug-in compatibility ([[E01]]). The Artcraft catalog establishes that apps exist; it does not establish parity or adoption ([[E02]]). Adobe’s Q3 FY2026 disclosure shows revenue growth and ARR before the episode; it does not isolate exposed seats or post-event impact ([[E08]]). The notebook’s own gap register says no independent production-parity benchmark exists ([[E10]]). [Inference] Therefore the binding uncertainty is capability: without a professional-workflow substitute, adoption, seat economics and valuation transmission are unsupported regardless of virality.

* What to acquire
A public release from an independent lab, design/photography publication or academic group that:
1. Recruits consenting professional users with a pre-registered task list.
2. Tests original PSD, PRPROJ, LR catalog and related files.
3. Measures task completion without Adobe fallback, median time-to-finish, fidelity/round-trip integrity, crashes, colour management, plug-in compatibility and collaboration.
4. Publishes raw scores, sample size, selection bias and limitations.
This matches the notebook’s proposed independent production-workflow trials and keeps vendor claims separate from measurement.

* Falsifiable test
Capability leg falsified if the published trial shows any of: less than 80% of pre-registered tasks completed without Adobe fallback; median time-to-finish worse than 1.5× Adobe; file round-trip corruption or material fidelity loss above a pre-declared threshold; or missing required plug-ins/colour management for professional workflows. If it meets those thresholds, the capability gate opens and research should move to paid-adoption and economics. A single demo, screenshot or download count cannot satisfy the test ([[E01]], [[E02]], [[E03]]).

* Strongest counterargument
Even a passing capability test is not a short signal. It would not prove paid migration, cancellation, pricing pressure or valuation reset. Enterprise procurement, support, collaboration, legal durability and Adobe’s own AI bundling may contain the threat. Adobe reported +13% y/y revenue and $27.50bn ending ARR before the episode; no verified cancellation cohort, borrow quote, options surface or catalyst exists ([[E08]], [[E10]]). The DeepSeek analogy is a mechanism comparison, not a calibrated Adobe forecast ([[E06]], [[E07]], [[E09]]). [Inference] So the highest-value observation decides whether the thesis deserves continued research, not whether to initiate a short.

* Missing data / next steps
Still unknown: independent production-parity benchmark, paying-user displacement cohort, exposed-seat revenue bridge, dated catalyst, borrow/recall economics and options positioning ([[E10]]). If the independent trial is unavailable, the next useful public observation is Adobe’s next scheduled quarterly disclosure with subscriber/ARR detail, read only as adoption evidence and not as a price forecast. Keep the decision on watch; no trade on this signal alone.
