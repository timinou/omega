* Priority
The next design priority is the ordinary editing and prompting experience:
structural and component selection, one request over selected targets, local
replacement, and switching between inline components and split source/component
views. See experience.bl.org. Rich evidence is a later extension to that flow.

* Retained proposal
Extend candidate check names into structured records naming the exact source,
property, assumptions, outcome and diagnostic or counterexample. Distinguish
passed, failed, unsupported, unknown, timeout and error.

An evidence inspector can open from a check next to the relevant object, keeping
the source and music in context. Evidence values should have ordinary commands:
select the originating expression, prepare an evaluation of a failing input,
and request a bounded repair. Do not automatically send arbitrary witnesses to
audio. Preserve previous attempts and their evidence as inspectable versions.

For the existing scalar-helper probe, root 97 and offset 0 reproduce the
unclamped pitch helper's out-of-range result. This is evidence about that helper
under integer assumptions, not proof of a collection-valued melody or timing.

* Integration outline
- Use request-owned staging and revision-owned results.
- Gather actual codebase and browser capability facts.
- Run name/arity, signature/type, compiler and musical checks outside audio work.
- Add supported scalar solver properties with explicit assumptions.
- Attach diagnostics and witnesses to ordinary repair requests.
- Record source, requests, checks and decisions before introducing memoization.

* Experience constraint
Do not introduce mandatory evidence panels, proof review or additional approval
steps into the immediate editing design. The musical object and local request
remain the center of the experience. Existing analysis findings and limits are
recorded in research.bl.md; the executable probe remains prototype2.bl.
